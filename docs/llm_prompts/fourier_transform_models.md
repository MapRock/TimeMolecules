Prompt: How can Fast Fourier Transform models fit into Time Molecules as event-derived frequency-domain models, and how can their spectral components help identify common drivers across signals?

Abstract: FFT analysis can be handled from the Time Molecules Event Ensemble as a traditional time-based analysis over a regular numeric signal. Instead of modeling transitions between multiple events, an FFT-style model usually selects one event type, sets `ByCase = 0`, chooses a numeric event-level metric such as temperature, humidity, vibration, closing price, or sales amount, and retrieves the ordered values over a time window. The signal can then be decomposed into spectral components such as frequency, amplitude, phase, period, and rank. A future implementation could store these results as `Models.ModelType = 'FFT'`, with model items representing the dominant components. The value is not merely detecting cycles inside one signal, but comparing spectral components across signals. If different signals share similar frequency, amplitude, and phase, they may have a common driver, such as seasonality, operating schedules, environmental cycles, market behavior, or machine behavior. Shared components are clues for human or AI reasoning, not proof of causation.
