
Prompt: How do Bayesian-style conditional probabilities work in Time Molecules, and how can they measure the likelihood of one event sequence given another?

Abstract: Bayesian probabilities in Time Molecules are best understood as Bayesian-style conditional probabilities between event sequences. The current implementation does not perform full Bayesian updating with dynamically revised priors; instead, it calculates relationships such as `P(B | A)`, where Sequence A and Sequence B may each be a single event or a multi-event sequence. For example, it can answer questions such as “Given `arrive`, what is the probability of `drinks`?” or “Given `greeted,seated,intro`, what is the probability of `bigtip`?” The result is based on counts such as how often Sequence A occurred, how often Sequence B occurred, and how often both occurred in the same grouping context. These probabilities can be persisted in `BayesianProbabilities`, allowing Time Molecules to cache reusable sequence-given-sequence relationships. Markov models describe event-flow transitions, while Bayesian probabilities describe broader conditional relationships between meaningful story fragments, supporting outcome analysis, anomaly investigation, and future hidden-state reasoning.
