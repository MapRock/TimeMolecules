Prompt: How are Markov models in Time Molecules partially additive, and how can compatible non-overlapping models be combined without rereading the underlying events?

Abstract: Markov models in Time Molecules are not fully additive in the simple OLAP sense, because values such as probabilities, averages, standard deviations, and coefficients of variation cannot simply be added across models. However, important parts of a model are partially additive when the models are logically compatible and non-overlapping. The clearest case is when two models have the same event set, transforms, metric, case/event filters, and handling of cases, but cover different non-overlapping date ranges. In that situation, segment-level row counts and metric sums can be added, while derived values such as averages and probabilities can be recomputed from the combined counts and sums. This allows pre-created Markov models to function like time-side BI aggregates: reusable summaries that avoid repeatedly scanning large event populations. The essential requirement is to prove compatibility and non-overlap from model parameters, date ranges, and property metadata, without retrieving the raw events.
